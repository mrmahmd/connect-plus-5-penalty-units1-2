## Lesson map and learning objectives

### Unit 1, Lesson 1: A Great Trip! (pp. 4–5)

- Identify six ecosystems and living and non-living things.
- Explain how mangrove roots and leaves help the ecosystem.
- Find key information in Heba’s school-trip report.

### Unit 1, Lesson 2: Language: Relative Clauses (pp. 6–7)

- Use who for people, which for things and where for places.
- Complete and correct relative clauses.

### Unit 1, Lesson 3: A Rainforest Ecosystem (pp. 8–9)

- Recognise rainforest vocabulary and how living things interact.
- Distinguish adjectives from abstract nouns: beauty, luck, patience and success.

### Unit 1, Lesson 4: Story: In the Mangrove Tree (pp. 10–11)

- Identify the animals, their feelings and the problem in the story.
- Explain how replanting and pollination help the forest recover.

### Unit 1, Lesson 5: I Have a Healthy Heart (pp. 12–13)

- Use heart, blood, nutrients, roots, sunlight and energy accurately.
- Compare how plants and humans receive what they need.
- Identify the healthy habits described in the lesson.

### Unit 1, Lesson 7: Writing: Linking Words (pp. 16–17)

- Use and to add information, but for contrast, because for a reason and so for a result.
- Recognise how a short paragraph connects healthy habits.

### Unit 1, Lesson 8: Story: Waiting for the Rain (pp. 18–19)

- Explain the connection between rain, grass, rabbits and lions in the story.
- Interpret can, cannot, might, will and must in context.

### Unit 2, Lesson 1: Wonders of Africa (pp. 22–23)

- Identify natural wonders and match geographical words to definitions.
- Find details about Ngorongoro Crater, Kilimanjaro, Victoria Falls and the Sahara.

### Unit 2, Lesson 2: Language: Present Perfect (pp. 24–25)

- Form affirmative, negative and question forms with have/has and a past participle.
- Recognise regular and irregular participles.
- Distinguish finished past events from experiences and actions continuing to the present.

### Unit 2, Lesson 3: Man-made Wonders of Africa (pp. 26–27)

- Distinguish natural and man-made wonders.
- Use monument, ruins, columns, centuries and cave paintings.
- Find information about the places described in the reading.

### Unit 2, Lesson 4: Story: The Storm-chaser (pp. 28–29)

- Identify extreme weather and explain Kamal’s experiences.
- Use present perfect forms in a story context.
- Sequence the problem, actions and outcome in the story.

### Unit 2, Lesson 5: Volcanoes (pp. 30–31)

- Define lava, soil, erupt, mud flow and ash.
- Extract facts from the poem about Java.
- Recognise how the poem connects volcanoes with the soil.

### Unit 2, Lesson 7: Writing a Description (pp. 34–35)

- Identify location, age, appearance and formation in a description.
- Use geographical and descriptive vocabulary.
- Recognise the information needed in a tourist brochure.

### Unit 2, Lesson 8: Story: A Balloon Ride (pp. 36–37)

- Identify the events and message in Mariam’s balloon story.
- Use basket, flame, gentle, light/lit and ribbon.
- Interpret descriptive language and recognise past and present perfect forms.

## Assessment design

112 authored questions: 8 per retained lesson; each lesson has 4 multiple-choice, 2 true/false and 2 correction questions. All questions have an answer, explanatory feedback, skill category, source pages and lesson ID. Distractors are authored assessment choices; they are not presented as quotations from the book. No missing listening transcript has been invented. Listening-linked vocabulary is assessed only where the printed page provides the information.

The game checks recognition, vocabulary, grammar, content comprehension and selected writing conventions. It does not claim to assess free speaking, listening without supplied audio, extended composition or independent research. These remain classroom learning objectives.

Quick Match: one question from every selected lesson. Cup Run: two questions from every selected lesson. Full Training: every matching bank question. Questions and options are shuffled. Across all-skills quick matches, the three question types and the three categories are guaranteed. Skill-focused games restrict only the skill; coverage is shown on screen.

Every correct answer leads to a goal; every incorrect answer is saved by the keeper. Aim direction changes the animation, not the academic mark. First-attempt answers are locked and recorded. Corrections do not rewrite the mark. Match XP includes streak bonuses; accuracy is always correct answers divided by questions. Retry Misses is explicitly a new practice attempt.

Student details and current progress stay in sessionStorage in the current browser tab. No student data is uploaded by the game to a server or GitHub.
